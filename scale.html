Each circle is one county, sized by its votes: its Yes votes go in the left pan and its No votes in the right, on one shared scale.

How the scale works

Counties drop in from largest to smallest. Everything is simulated: the circles fall and pile up, the beam turns under the weight that has landed in each pan, and the pans swing on their chains. As on a real balance, the tilt grows in proportion to the difference in weight; this one is sensitive enough that a 65–35 result holds it against its stops. The dial at the top is geared to the beam and graduated in vote shares, so the needle reads each side’s percentage; the reading is exact once the scale comes to rest. On measures that need 55% to pass, a notch marks that share.

Votes by county

Turn on Edit votes to change any count and see how the scale responds. Changing a county’s total keeps its Yes share; changing its Yes share (slider or percentage) keeps its total. As you edit, the county’s circles grow or shrink, and the beam and dial follow the new weights. Edits aren’t saved, and picking another measure starts again from the reported results.
