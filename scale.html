Weighing the vote

Yes votesNo votes

Each circle is one county, sized by its votes: its Yes votes go in the left pan and its No votes in the right. Both pans use the same scale, so the circles on each side add up to that side’s statewide votes. Counties drop in from largest to smallest. Everything is simulated: the circles fall and pile up, the beam turns under the weight that has landed in each pan, and the pans swing on their chains. As on a real balance, the tilt grows in proportion to the difference in weight; this one is sensitive enough that a 60–40 result holds it against its stops.
